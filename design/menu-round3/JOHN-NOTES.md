# John's notes after trying the menus (21/10, in his words, lightly trimmed). These OVERRIDE the rankers' advice.

**Shadow Strings (the puppet):** "I liked the physics based toy in the puppet one and the visual simplicity of the menu.
It was a bit wonky in that the puppet gets immediately tangled and the numbers on the strings aren't anchored to their
labels, but it was the most pleasing immediately visually. Not sure about the functionality of the menu."

**Three Inks:** "This had the most solid and immediately understandable UI. I liked the colors helping categorize the
talks. It was too visually busy for me though. I think this one has the most potential, but needs work to make things
immediately parsable and limit the amount of info on screen at any time."

"Both could be great but aren't there yet."

## What follows
- Keep what he liked: the physics toy and the resting simplicity (Shadow Strings); the clear UI and colour-as-category
  (Three Inks).
- Do NOT add information to the resting screens. The rankers' lever "show all eight verdict names at rest" is
  withdrawn where it makes a screen busier: the eight verdicts may sit one step in. The test is: at any moment the
  screen shows one clear thing and a small number of items (about 5-6 rows, a few controls), immediately parsable.
- Shadow Strings: the puppet must not tangle; each string's number must be anchored to its own label.
- Three Inks: cut the visual busyness hard; limit what is on screen at any time.
